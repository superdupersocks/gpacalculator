Section Title

GPA stands for Grade Point Average and represents your overall academic performance. It's calculated by converting letter grades to numbers on a standard scale, then averaging those numbers across all your courses. Most U.S. colleges use a 4.0 scale where an A equals 4.0, B equals 3.0, and so on.

  • Enter your course names in the calculator
  • Select the letter grade you received for each course
  • Input the number of credit hours for each class
  • Your GPA will calculate automatically as you type
  1. List all courses from your transcript or syllabus
  2. Convert each letter grade to its corresponding grade point value
  3. Multiply each grade point by the course credit hours to get quality points
  4. Add up all quality points and all credit hours
  5. Divide total quality points by total credit hours to get your GPA

Key Concepts

Important Terms

GPA (Grade Point Average)

A numerical calculation that represents the average of all grades earned in courses, typically on a scale of 0.0 to 4.0. The GPA is calculated by dividing the total number of grade points earned by the total number of credit hours attempted.

⚠️ Important

Always use your official transcript when calculating GPA for applications. Different schools may have slightly different grading scales or policies for pass/fail courses, withdrawals, and repeated classes.

💡 Tip

Focus on earning A's in high-credit courses to maximize your GPA improvement. A single A in a 4-credit class has more impact than an A in a 1-credit elective.

GPA = Total Grade Points ÷ Total Credits

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between semester GPA and cumulative GPA?

Semester GPA reflects your performance in one term only. Cumulative GPA includes all completed semesters and is the number colleges rely on for academic standing, major eligibility, honors, and graduation requirements.

What is the difference between weighted GPA and unweighted GPA?

An unweighted GPA uses the standard 4.0 scale. A weighted GPA rewards tougher courses like Honors or AP by giving extra points – for example, an A in an AP class can count as 5.0 instead of 4.0.

What are credit hours in GPA calculation?

Credit hours show how much a class is worth. A 4-credit course carries more weight in your GPA than a 1-credit elective, so the grade you earn matters more.
