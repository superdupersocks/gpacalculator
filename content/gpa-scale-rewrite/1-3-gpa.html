Is a 1.3 GPA good?

No. A 1.3 is well below average: D+ work, about 67–69%, against a national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study). It is under the 2.0 that many colleges set as a minimum, so raising it is the priority.

In high school

Most four-year colleges will not admit a student below 2.0. Use the time you have left in high school to raise it; community colleges are open-admission and a common route to a four-year degree.

In college

In college a GPA below 2.0 usually means academic probation, which can affect financial aid. Talk to an academic advisor about a plan.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 1.3. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 1.3 GPA means for college

With a 1.3 most four-year colleges will not admit you directly. Community colleges are open-admission, and many students transfer to a four-year college after earning good grades there.

If you are still in high school, every semester of better grades counts. Ask your counselor about credit recovery for classes you failed.

What helps

Now that you know where you stand it’s a good idea to think about ways to raise your GPA.  

  1. Focus: First of all, you should focus on areas in which you are struggling.  Identify subjects that give you trouble and work on bringing those grades up.  It’s a good idea to identify your toughest classes and find ways to get help.  
  2. Study regularly: You can hire a tutor or join a study group.  Block off time to study every day - be consistent and work hard giving each assignment the time it needs.  
  3. Stay Organized: Find a way to keep yourself organized - knowing when assignments are due and turning them in on time will go a long way.  

Know your limits: It’s also important to know your limits.  Don’t take unnecessary courses you predict will be a challenge.

1.3 GPA – Frequently asked questions

What does a 1.3 GPA indicate?

A 1.3 GPA indicates significantly below-average academic performance, often equating to receiving 'D' grades in most courses. This GPA level highlights profound challenges in understanding coursework and underscores an urgent need for targeted academic support and strategies aimed at improvement.

Is a 1.3 GPA good enough for college admissions?

A 1.3 GPA is well below the average admissions standards for most colleges, presenting significant obstacles to gaining admission through standard processes. However, some institutions might offer alternative pathways or special programs for students who demonstrate potential for academic growth. To bolster your application, focus on showcasing areas of personal development, meaningful extracurricular participation, and obtaining supportive letters of recommendation that speak to your potential for success.

How can I improve a 1.3 GPA?

Improving a 1.3 GPA requires a concentrated effort to achieve higher grades in future classes. Consider retaking courses in which you've previously performed poorly to boost your overall GPA. Adopting rigorous study routines, engaging in academic tutoring or support programs, and prioritizing your education are crucial steps toward academic recovery.

What scholarship opportunities are available with a 1.3 GPA?

While a 1.3 GPA significantly narrows the scope for academic merit-based scholarships, financial aid opportunities that evaluate a broader spectrum of qualifications may still be available. Look for scholarships that emphasize community involvement, personal growth, overcoming adversity, or specific talents and interests. Some scholarships are specifically designed to support students who are determined to improve their academic standing and demonstrate the potential for success despite initial setbacks.
