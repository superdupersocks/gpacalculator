Is a 1.6 GPA good?

No. A 1.6 is well below average: C− work, about 71%, against a national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study). It is under the 2.0 that many colleges set as a minimum, so raising it is the priority.

In high school

Most four-year colleges will not admit a student below 2.0. Use the time you have left in high school to raise it; community colleges are open-admission and a common route to a four-year degree.

In college

In college a GPA below 2.0 usually means academic probation, which can affect financial aid. Talk to an academic advisor about a plan.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 1.6. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 1.6 GPA means for college

With a 1.6 most four-year colleges will not admit you directly. Community colleges are open-admission, and many students transfer to a four-year college after earning good grades there.

If you are still in high school, every semester of better grades counts. Ask your counselor about credit recovery for classes you failed.

Sophomore

You are about halfway through, and raising a 1.6 is still realistic. Focus first on the classes where you are closest to the next grade, and turn in every assignment. Colleges also notice an upward trend.

Junior

Junior-year grades are the last full year most colleges see before you apply, so they count. Even if your overall GPA moves only a little, strong junior grades show an upward trend. Ask your counselor about retaking classes you failed.

Senior

Your GPA will not move much before applications, so plan around it: apply to colleges whose requirements you meet, including community colleges, and keep your senior grades up, because colleges see them and some offers depend on them.

What helps

Now that you know where you stand it’s a good idea to think about ways to raise your GPA.  

  1. Get Organized: Making sure you turn your assignments in on time and are prepared for tests is an easy way to raise your GPA by earning on time points and being ready on test day.  Make a list of all of your assignments and plan out the time you’ll need to complete them.  
  2. Work Together: If there’s a class you really struggle with seek out help.  Even just an accountability partner to check in and make sure you’ve studied can go a long way to raising your GPA.  If you’re really struggling you may want to join a study group or even find a tutor.  
  3. Set Small Goals: It can feel overwhelming to raise your GPA so start small.  Choose one class or even one assignment and do everything you can to raise the grade.  Once you conquer one low grade you’ll feel more prepared to tackle the rest.

1.6 GPA – Frequently asked questions

What does a 1.6 GPA indicate?

A 1.6 GPA signifies below-average academic performance, usually corresponding to a 'C-' average in coursework. This GPA indicates considerable room for improvement in academic skills and knowledge comprehension.

Is a 1.6 GPA good enough for college admissions?

A 1.6 GPA is below the typical admissions standards for most colleges, making traditional admission challenging. However, some institutions may consider students with lower GPAs through alternative admissions programs or on a provisional basis. Highlighting extracurricular achievements, personal growth, and obtaining strong letters of recommendation can help support your application.

How can I improve a 1.6 GPA?

Improving a 1.6 GPA requires a dedicated approach to achieving better grades in upcoming classes, possibly retaking courses where grades were particularly low. Key strategies include adopting effective study habits, seeking out academic support or tutoring, and prioritizing academic work to enhance performance.

What scholarship opportunities are available with a 1.6 GPA?

Scholarship opportunities for a 1.6 GPA may be limited, especially for those based on academic merit. However, there are scholarships that consider other factors, such as community involvement, personal achievements, leadership qualities, or overcoming significant challenges. Additionally, some scholarships are specifically designed to support students who have shown the potential for academic improvement and are committed to achieving their educational goals.
