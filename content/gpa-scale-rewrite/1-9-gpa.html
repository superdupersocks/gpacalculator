Is a 1.9 GPA good?

No. A 1.9 is well below average: C work, about 74%, against a national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study). It is under the 2.0 that many colleges set as a minimum, so raising it is the priority.

In high school

Most four-year colleges will not admit a student below 2.0. Use the time you have left in high school to raise it; community colleges are open-admission and a common route to a four-year degree.

In college

In college a GPA below 2.0 usually means academic probation, which can affect financial aid. Talk to an academic advisor about a plan.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 1.9. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 1.9 GPA means for college

With a 1.9 most four-year colleges will not admit you directly. Community colleges are open-admission, and many students transfer to a four-year college after earning good grades there.

If you are still in high school, every semester of better grades counts. Ask your counselor about credit recovery for classes you failed.

Sophomore

You are about halfway through, and raising a 1.9 is still realistic. Focus first on the classes where you are closest to the next grade, and turn in every assignment. Colleges also notice an upward trend.

Junior

Junior-year grades are the last full year most colleges see before you apply, so they count. Even if your overall GPA moves only a little, strong junior grades show an upward trend. Ask your counselor about retaking classes you failed.

Senior

Your GPA will not move much before applications, so plan around it: apply to colleges whose requirements you meet, including community colleges, and keep your senior grades up, because colleges see them and some offers depend on them.

What helps

Now that you know where you stand it’s a good idea to consider things you might do to raise your GPA.  

  1. Identify your Struggles:  The first step to improving your grades is identifying your trouble spots.  Start with the class that’s the most difficult for you and work to improve that grade first.  
  2. Choose Your Classes Wisely: It can be overwhelming to choose classes each semester, but if possible, don’t overload your schedule with difficult classes.  You’ll be much more likely to get a good GPA if you steer away from advanced classes and take a manageable course load.  
  3. Two Heads are Better than One: Sometimes a study group can be just the accountability you need to get your grades on track. Make a group with others in your class or reach out to a counselor or teacher to find out if any groups are already up and running.  

1.9 GPA – Frequently asked questions

What does a 1.9 GPA indicate?

A 1.9 GPA indicates that your academic performance has been below average, with grades typically falling in the 'C-' range. This GPA demonstrates limited understanding of your coursework and suggests significant areas for academic development and focus.

Is a 1.9 GPA good enough for college admissions?

A 1.9 GPA is considered low for most college admissions but may still meet the criteria for certain institutions, particularly those that offer conditional admission programs or have more inclusive admissions policies. Enhancing your application with evidence of strong character, determination, extracurricular achievements, and persuasive recommendations can help offset the academic shortfall.

How can I improve a 1.9 GPA?

Improving a 1.9 GPA will require concerted effort to achieve higher grades in your subsequent courses. Consider retaking classes where you've received especially low marks. Focusing on developing stronger study strategies, seeking out academic support and tutoring, and dedicating adequate time to your studies are essential steps towards raising your GPA.

What scholarship opportunities are available with a 1.9 GPA?

Scholarship opportunities for a 1.9 GPA may be limited, particularly those based on academic merit. However, scholarships that evaluate personal achievements, community service, leadership experiences, or personal challenges faced may be accessible. Some organizations and institutions also offer scholarships aimed at students who demonstrate significant potential for success despite previous academic struggles, recognizing the value of growth and the capacity for improvement.
