A 2.0 GPA equals a C average, about 73–76%. It is the minimum many colleges require for admission or good academic standing, and well below the national average of around 3.0.

  • Letter grade: C
  • Percentage: about 73–76%
  • National average: 3.11 for 2019 high school graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study)
  • Scale: unweighted 4.0

Is a 2.0 GPA good?

A 2.0 is below average. It works out to C work, about 73–76%, against a national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study). It meets the minimum at some colleges but leaves little room.

In high school

Many four-year colleges look for at least a 2.5, so this GPA limits your choices. Community colleges and open-admission schools remain open to you while you work on it.

In college

In college you are in good standing (usually 2.0 or above), but some majors, scholarships and graduate programs require more.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 2.0. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 2.0 GPA means for college

A 2.0 meets the minimum at some four-year colleges and at all open-admission and community colleges. Many colleges look for 2.5 or higher, so your choices are limited for now.

Raising your GPA before you apply, or starting at a community college and transferring after a strong first year, opens more options.

Freshman

Most of high school is still ahead of you, so a 2.0 now can change a lot. Every semester of better grades pulls your average up; the table below shows how many strong classes it takes to reach a 2.5.

Sophomore

You are about halfway through, and raising a 2.0 is still realistic. Focus first on the classes where you are closest to the next grade, and turn in every assignment. Colleges also notice an upward trend.

Junior

Junior-year grades are the last full year most colleges see before you apply, so they count. Even if your overall GPA moves only a little, strong junior grades show an upward trend. Ask your counselor about retaking classes you failed.

Senior

Your GPA will not move much before applications, so plan around it: apply to colleges whose requirements you meet, including community colleges, and keep your senior grades up, because colleges see them and some offers depend on them.

What helps

Now that you know what a 2.0 GPA means, it’s a good idea to think about what you can do to raise it.  

  1. Get Organized: Turning every assignment in on time is a great way to make sure you don’t lose unnecessary points.  Take a calendar and write in all of the due dates for each assignment.  Commit yourself to turning in completed assignments on time every time.   
  2. Prioritize: If raising your GPA is important to you, make it a priority.  Set aside time to study before socializing, watching TV or doing other things that are “just for fun.”  Prioritizing your studies will be well worth it in the end!

Ask for Help: Don’t be afraid to ask for help!  Seek out a school counselor, teacher or teacher’s aid and ask for help on things that you just can’t figure out on your own. 

2.0 GPA – Frequently asked questions

What does a 2.0 GPA indicate?

A 2.0 GPA indicates a 'C' average, suggesting that your academic performance has been average, meeting the basic requirements of your coursework. This GPA level points to a foundational understanding of your subjects, with ample room for growth and improvement.

Is a 2.0 GPA good enough for college admissions?

A 2.0 GPA is on the lower end for college admissions but may suffice for admission into some colleges, particularly those that employ a holistic review process and place less emphasis on GPA alone. To strengthen your application, it's beneficial to highlight extracurricular activities, personal achievements, and strong letters of recommendation that showcase your abilities and potential beyond academics.

How can I improve a 2.0 GPA?

Improving a 2.0 GPA requires a focused effort to earn higher grades in future courses. Retaking courses in which you've scored low to improve those grades can also be beneficial. Adopting effective study habits, utilizing academic support services, and prioritizing your academic responsibilities are key strategies to enhance your academic performance.

What scholarship opportunities are available with a 2.0 GPA?

While a 2.0 GPA may limit eligibility for competitive merit-based scholarships, there are financial aid opportunities available that consider factors beyond GPA. Scholarships focusing on community involvement, leadership qualities, specific talents, or experiences that demonstrate resilience and potential for success can be viable options. Additionally, some scholarships are specifically designed to support students who show promise for improvement and success despite facing academic challenges.
