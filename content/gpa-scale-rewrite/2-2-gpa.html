Is a 2.2 GPA good?

A 2.2 is below average. It works out to C+ work, about 77%, against a national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study). It meets the minimum at some colleges but leaves little room.

In high school

Many four-year colleges look for at least a 2.5, so this GPA limits your choices. Community colleges and open-admission schools remain open to you while you work on it.

In college

In college you are in good standing (usually 2.0 or above), but some majors, scholarships and graduate programs require more.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 2.2. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 2.2 GPA means for college

A 2.2 meets the minimum at some four-year colleges and at all open-admission and community colleges. Many colleges look for 2.5 or higher, so your choices are limited for now.

Raising your GPA before you apply, or starting at a community college and transferring after a strong first year, opens more options.

Freshman

Most of high school is still ahead of you, so a 2.2 now can change a lot. Every semester of better grades pulls your average up; the table below shows how many strong classes it takes to reach a 2.5.

Sophomore

You are about halfway through, and raising a 2.2 is still realistic. Focus first on the classes where you are closest to the next grade, and turn in every assignment. Colleges also notice an upward trend.

Junior

Junior-year grades are the last full year most colleges see before you apply, so they count. Even if your overall GPA moves only a little, strong junior grades show an upward trend. Ask your counselor about retaking classes you failed.

Senior

Your GPA will not move much before applications, so plan around it: apply to colleges whose requirements you meet, including community colleges, and keep your senior grades up, because colleges see them and some offers depend on them.

What helps

Now that you know where you stand it’s a good idea to think about ways to raise your GPA.  

  1. Dedication: Raising your GPA takes hard work and it won’t happen overnight. Make a plan and stick to it, and you will see results! Commit yourself to turning in every assignment on time and completed in full. 
  2. Pick your battles: Distance yourself from any outside distractions and minimize extra commitments until you meet your GPA goals.
  3. Keep practicing: Ask your professor/teacher for extra practice assignments to help you master any concepts that confuse you or find independent practice resources to help build your skills.

2.2 GPA – Frequently asked questions

What does a 2.2 GPA indicate?

A 2.2 GPA signifies a 'C' average, showing that your academic performance is below average. This GPA reflects a basic grasp of coursework, highlighting a need for significant improvement in academic efforts and understanding.

Is a 2.2 GPA good enough for college admissions?

A 2.2 GPA falls below the average requirements for many colleges but might meet the criteria for certain institutions, especially those that consider the entirety of an applicant's profile beyond GPA. Strengthening your application with notable extracurricular achievements, compelling essays, and strong recommendations can help balance a lower GPA in the admissions process.

How can I improve a 2.2 GPA?

Improving a 2.2 GPA involves dedicating effort to score higher in future classes and possibly revisiting courses where you've earned low grades to improve them. Prioritizing your academic work, adopting effective study techniques, and utilizing available academic resources and support are key steps towards enhancing your GPA.

What scholarship opportunities are available with a 2.2 GPA?

While a 2.2 GPA may restrict eligibility for highly competitive academic scholarships, there are still opportunities for financial aid that consider a wide range of criteria. Scholarships that focus on community service, leadership roles, specific personal achievements, or unique talents can be accessible. Additionally, some scholarships are designed to support students who have faced challenges but show potential for future success.
