Is a 2.5 GPA good?

A 2.5 is a little below average. It means B− work, about 80%, while 2019 high school graduates averaged 3.11 (NAEP High School Transcript Study). It is not a problem for many colleges, but it narrows your options at selective ones.

In high school

It meets the minimum at many four-year colleges, but you will be below the typical admitted student at selective ones. Improving your grades this year is worth the effort.

In college

In college it is under the 3.0 that many scholarships and graduate programs require, but well above the 2.0 needed for good standing.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 2.5. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 2.5 GPA means for college

With a 2.5 you can get into many four-year colleges, especially regional public universities and less selective private colleges. Selective colleges are a stretch.

Colleges notice an upward trend, so better grades this year help. Community college followed by a transfer is another proven route.

Freshman

Most of high school is still ahead of you, so a 2.5 now can change a lot. Every semester of better grades pulls your average up; the table below shows how many strong classes it takes to reach a 3.0.

Sophomore

You are about halfway through, and raising a 2.5 is still realistic. Focus first on the classes where you are closest to the next grade, and turn in every assignment. Colleges also notice an upward trend.

Junior

Junior-year grades are the last full year most colleges see before you apply, so they count. Even if your overall GPA moves only a little, strong junior grades show an upward trend. Ask your counselor about retaking classes you failed.

Senior

Your GPA will not move much before applications, so plan around it: apply to colleges whose requirements you meet, including community colleges, and keep your senior grades up, because colleges see them and some offers depend on them.

What helps

  • Turn in everything: complete, on-time work is the quickest way to stop losing points.
  • Get help in your hardest class: tutoring or a study group early in the term.
  • Ask about retakes: some schools replace or average a retaken grade.

2.5 GPA – Frequently asked questions

How can I raise my 2.5 GPA? 

Now that you know where you stand it’s a good idea to come up with a plan to raise your 2.5 GPA so that you can achieve your goals.    

  1. Study First: Students often have a lot going on, but if you want to raise your GPA you need to make your school work your first priority.  Plan studying into your schedule and don’t let anything else distract you.       
  2. Focus on Your Struggles: Even one low grade can really bring down your GPA so give extra time and attention on your hardest class.  You may even want to touch base with your teacher to find out what you can do to bring up your lowest grade.    
  3. On Time Every Time: Making sure you turn in every single assignment complete and on time is an easy way to ensure you’re not losing points.  Make a list for yourself and commit to turning in every assignment complete and on time every time.
