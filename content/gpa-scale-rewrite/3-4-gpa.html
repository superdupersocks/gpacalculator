Is a 3.4 GPA good?

Yes. A 3.4 is a good GPA: B+ work, about 89%, and comfortably above the national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study).

In high school

It shows steady, above-average work and keeps a wide range of four-year colleges open. Strong courses and an upward trend make it even more convincing.

In college

In college it keeps you in good standing for most scholarships and clears the 3.0 that many graduate programs and employers ask for.

Weighted or unweighted?

These figures are for the unweighted 4.0 scale. If your school gives extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, your weighted GPA can be higher than 3.4. Colleges usually recalculate GPAs their own way, so they see both. Work out yours with the Weighted GPA calculator.

What a 3.4 GPA means for college

A 3.4 fits the typical range at a large number of four-year colleges and is competitive at many selective ones. The most selective schools usually admit students with higher GPAs, so treat them as reaches.

Check each college's published GPA range (often on its admissions page or Common Data Set) and build a balanced list around it.

What helps

  • Find the weakest class: moving one B+ to an A− lifts your GPA more than polishing classes you already ace.
  • Use office hours: ask teachers what separates your work from an A.
  • Plan ahead: keep a calendar of tests and deadlines so nothing is done last-minute.

3.4 GPA – Frequently asked questions

How can I raise my 3.4 GPA? 

Raising a 3.4 GPA may be a bit of a challenge, as it is already quite high.  But if it’s important to you, make a plan and you can make it happen!  

  1. Look Things Over:  It’s easy to make mistakes with so much work on your plate so take time to look each assignment over after you’ve completed it and before you turn it in.  A little extra time to catch any errors will pay off in the long run with a higher grade.   
  2. Study First: You likely have a lot on your plate, but right now isn’t the time to take on anything extra.  Set aside time to study before playing sports, socializing or even volunteering.  If you’re working hard to raise your GPA you need to make study time a priority.      
  3. Focus on Your Hardest Class: With an above average GPA even one single low grade can really bring you down.  Spend extra time on your hardest class.  You may even want to touch base with the teacher to see if there’s anything you can do to further raise your grade. 

What does a 3.4 GPA indicate?

A 3.4 GPA is indicative of solid 'B+' performance, showing that you have consistently achieved above-average grades in your courses. It suggests a good understanding of your study material and a strong commitment to your academic responsibilities.

Is a 3.4 GPA good enough for college admissions?

Yes, a 3.4 GPA is considered good for college admissions across many institutions. It demonstrates steady academic achievement. While more selective colleges might look for higher GPAs, a 3.4 is competitive, especially when paired with a strong overall application, including extracurricular activities and personal essays.

How can I improve a 3.4 GPA?

Improving a 3.4 GPA involves focusing on earning higher grades in upcoming courses and possibly retaking classes where you've received lower grades, if allowed. Effective study habits, time management, and using available academic resources are crucial strategies for enhancing your GPA.

What scholarship opportunities are available with a 3.4 GPA?

A 3.4 GPA opens up numerous opportunities for scholarships, particularly those that recognize academic merit. While some highly competitive scholarships may require a higher GPA, many scholarships consider a 3.4 GPA to be competitive and reflective of a student's academic dedication.
