Is a 3.6 GPA good?

Yes. A 3.6 is a good GPA: A− work, about 90–92%, and comfortably above the national average of 3.11 for 2019 graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study).

In high school

It shows steady, above-average work and keeps a wide range of four-year colleges open. Strong courses and an upward trend make it even more convincing.

In college

In college it keeps you in good standing for most scholarships and clears the 3.0 that many graduate programs and employers ask for.

What a 3.6 GPA means for college

A 3.6 fits the typical range at a large number of four-year colleges and is competitive at many selective ones. The most selective schools usually admit students with higher GPAs, so treat them as reaches.

Check each college's published GPA range (often on its admissions page or Common Data Set) and build a balanced list around it.

Freshman

If you are a freshman, your GPA tends to change before your application is sent to the college but having a 3.6 GPA is a commendable feat. It is a sign that you are performing excellent and acquired high grades in most of your classes, and the next few years could see you perform as a better student and make a good impression on colleges with your academic development. As a freshman, if you can retain or go beyond the 3.6 GPA, you will be admitted by various selective schools. You may not have chosen colleges yet, and that's fine: use our GPA calculator each term to see where your GPA is heading.

Sophomore

Having passed through the first two years of high school with a robust GPA is an exceptional achievement. It is likely for your GPA to change slightly before you find admission to the college in your senior year but there will be no much difference if you continue to study at the same pace. Most of the best schools will consider you if you have a 3.6 GPA and this will offer you a range of choices when you want to select the college you want to study in. Junior

The process of getting admitted into schools should not be a problem for you if you have maintained a strong GPA throughout your high school except in rare cases where some schools are applying stringent measures. 3.6 GPA – Frequently asked questions

How can I raise my 3.6 GPA? 

Raising a 3.6 GPA may be challenging, as it is already quite high.  Choose your weakest areas and focus your attention on them.       

  1. Study Smart: You’re already quite good at studying or you wouldn’t have achieved such a high GPA in the first place.  If you’re looking to go the extra mile it may be wise to seek tips from a TA, join a study group or seek out the school tutoring center for some advice on taking your GPA to the next level. 
  2. Keep Up the Good Work: Once you see that your GPA is a 3.6 you may be tempted to breath a sigh of relief and take a well deserved break.  Don’t do it!  Keep working just as hard as ever to maintain and even raise your 3.6 GPA.  
  3. Talk to your Teachers: Teachers are the experts in their classes.  Seek out counsel from teachers who teach your most difficult classes.  Ask them for pointers and even for additional extra credit work.    

What does a 3.6 GPA indicate?

A 3.6 GPA demonstrates strong academic abilities, typically reflecting a mix of 'A' and 'B+' grades across your coursework. It signifies that you have consistently performed well above average in your studies.

Is a 3.6 GPA competitive for college admissions?

Yes, a 3.6 GPA is competitive for a wide range of colleges, indicating solid academic performance. While the most elite institutions may look for higher GPAs, a 3.6 can be strong, especially when complemented by strong extracurricular profiles and compelling essays.

Can I improve my 3.6 GPA?

Certainly, you can improve a 3.6 GPA by excelling in your remaining courses and potentially retaking any classes where you received lower grades. Utilizing academic resources, focusing on effective study habits, and seeking help when needed are key strategies for GPA improvement.

What impact does a 3.6 GPA have on scholarships?

A 3.6 GPA positions you well for many scholarship opportunities, especially those that focus on academic merit. It showcases to scholarship committees your commitment to academic excellence and can make you a strong candidate for financial awards.
