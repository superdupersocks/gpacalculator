3.8 GPA

A 3.8 GPA works out to an A- average, around 90–92%. That is an excellent record, comfortably above the typical 3.0, and it keeps highly selective colleges and merit scholarships within reach.

Updated October 1, 2026

  • Letter grade: A−
  • Percentage: about 90–92%
  • National average: 3.11 for 2019 high school graduates (NAEP High School Transcript Study)
  • Scale: unweighted 4.0

Is a 3.8 GPA good?

Yes. A 3.8 is an excellent GPA: it means A− work across your classes, about 90–92%, and it is well above the national average. High school graduates in 2019 averaged 3.11, according to the NAEP High School Transcript Study.

In high school

Admissions officers will read it as consistent, high-level work. At this level the rest of your application (course rigor, test scores if you send them, essays, activities) decides the most competitive admissions.

In college

In college a GPA this high stands out for scholarships, honors programs and graduate school, where 3.0 to 3.5 is a common minimum.

What a 3.8 GPA means for college

A 3.8 makes you a competitive applicant at most colleges, including many selective ones. At the most selective schools most admitted students have a GPA this high or higher, so your courses, essays and activities carry the decision.

Apply to a balanced list: a few reach schools, several where your GPA is at or above their typical range, and a couple of likely admits.

Freshman

If you are a freshman, your GPA tends to change before your application is sent to the college but having a 3.8 GPA is a commendable feat. It is a sign that you are performing excellent and acquired high grades in most of your classes, and the next few years could see you perform as a better student and make a good impression on colleges with your academic development. As a freshman, if you can retain or go beyond the 3.8 GPA, you will be admitted by various selective schools. You may not have chosen colleges yet, and that’s fine: use our GPA calculator each term to see where your GPA is heading.

Sophomore

Having passed through the first two years of high school with a robust GPA is an exceptional achievement. It is likely for your GPA to change slightly before you find admission to the college in your senior year but there will be no much difference if you continue to study at the same pace. Most of the best schools will consider you if you have a 3.8 GPA and this will offer you a range of choices when you want to select the college you want to study in. To see which grades would move it higher from here, try our Raise GPA calculator.

Junior

With a 3.8 GPA, you have succeeded in getting to the college application time. However, your GPA will not be changed at this point, and you should be applying to colleges with a GPA close to the value of a 3.8. It indicates that you would be counting on your eligibility to be admitted by various selective colleges. If you have schools in mind, compare your GPA with their published ranges, and use the Raise GPA calculator to see what this year’s grades can still change.

Senior

This is your senior year, and there are chances that you have applied to various schools with your GPA. The process of getting admitted into schools should not be a problem for you if you have maintained a strong GPA throughout your high school except in rare cases where some schools are applying stringent measures. If you are still finishing senior-year classes, the GPA calculator shows how your final grades will change the GPA colleges see.

What helps

Raising a 3.8 GPA is very challenging – so find areas where you can improve and focus your energy on perfecting those subjects. 

  1. Stay Ahead of the Game: Keep organized so you can prepare for assignments, projects, tests, and quizzes well in advance.
  2. Keep Exceptional Study Habits: Establish a routine for studying and pick dedicated study time every day.
  3. Go Above and Beyond: Ask your professors/teachers for extra work to build deep understanding and maybe even earn some extra credit!

3.8 GPA – Frequently asked questions

Is a 3.8 GPA good?

Yes. A 3.8 is an excellent GPA, well above the national average of about 3.1. It shows consistent A− work and is competitive for selective colleges, honors programs and merit scholarships.

What letter grade and percentage is a 3.8 GPA?

A 3.8 GPA is an A− average on the standard unweighted 4.0 scale, which is about 90–92%. Schools set their own cutoffs, so check how your school converts percentages to grade points.

Can I get into college with a 3.8 GPA?

Yes. A 3.8 makes you competitive at most colleges, including many selective ones. At the most selective schools a high GPA is expected, so course rigor, essays, recommendations and activities decide between strong applicants.

Is a 3.8 weighted GPA the same as a 3.8 unweighted GPA?

No. An unweighted GPA uses the 4.0 scale, where an A is 4.0. A weighted GPA adds extra points for Honors, AP or IB classes, so a 3.8 weighted GPA usually stands for a lower unweighted average than a 3.8 unweighted. Colleges often recalculate GPAs, so they see both.

How can I raise a 3.8 GPA to a 3.9?

With A’s in your next classes. If you have finished 8 equal-credit classes, about 8 more A’s take a 3.8 to a 3.9; after 16 classes it takes about 16. The earlier you start, the faster it moves, so focus first on the classes closest to the next grade up.

Is a 3.8 GPA good enough for scholarships?

Yes. A 3.8 clears the minimum GPA for most merit scholarships, which makes the rest of the application (essays, activities, test scores where required) the deciding factor.
